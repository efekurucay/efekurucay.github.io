Week 2 — Radio & Physical Layer

Yahya Efe Kuruçay · Akdeniz University, Computer Engineering · lab recorded 4 October 2026

Task

The week 2 Cooja lab: one Sky sender broadcasts a numbered packet every second, one Sky receiver logs the RSSI and LQI of each packet it gets. The receiver is moved to different distances, dragged across the range boundary, and the radio medium’s RX success ratio is reduced, to see how RSSI, LQI and the packet reception ratio (PRR) behave and why they are not interchangeable.

Experiment A — first packets

With the receiver inside the range, every TX seq=N of mote 1 is followed by RX seq=N RSSI=… LQI=37 on mote 2. While the receiver was still outside the green circle no RX lines appeared at all.

Experiment B — distance

The receiver was placed at three distances from the sender. The 10 m background grid gives the scale; the green circle is the 20 m transmission range.

RSSI falls with distance, but at each position every packet arrives and LQI stays at 37.

Experiment C — crossing the boundary

Dragging the receiver outside the green circle while the simulation runs: the sender keeps printing TX, the receiver goes silent. 15 consecutive packets (sequence numbers 493–507) were sent but never received. Back inside, reception resumes immediately.

Experiments D and E — reduced RX success ratio and PRR

The UDGM RX success ratio was set to 60 % (the default is 100 %) with the receiver about 10 m from the sender (half the range). Cooja then shows a reception probability of 89.2 % next to the receiver, not 60 %.

PRR counted from the Mote output over sequence numbers 700–719: 20 sent, 20 received, PRR = 1.00. In fact none of the 70 packets sent at this setting (680–749) was lost.

Why the results look like this

  • RSSI is linear in distance. UDGM computes the signal strength as a straight line from −10 dBm at the sender to −95 dBm at the edge of the range: RSSI = −10 − 85 × d / range (SS_STRONG and SS_WEAK in Cooja’s AbstractRadioMedium).
  • The printed RSSI is 45 dB too low. The lab code subtracts the CC2420 offset of 45, but Contiki’s driver has already applied it (cc2420_last_rssi = footer[0] + RSSI_OFFSET in dev/cc2420/cc2420.c). Adding 45 back gives −26, −53 and −72 dBm, which match the model for about 4, 10 and 15 m.
  • LQI carries no information here. It is 37 for every packet at every distance; UDGM does not model decoding quality.
  • The RX success ratio is not a fixed loss rate. In UDGM.java the reception probability is P = 1 − (d / range)² × (1 − RX ratio). At 10 m of a 20 m range that is 1 − 0.25 × 0.40 = 0.90, the 89.2 % Cooja displayed. The configured 60 % is reached only at the edge of the range; next to the sender it is almost 100 %.
  • No loss at 89 %. ContikiMAC repeats each broadcast for a whole wake-up interval, so a copy that fails the 89 % draw is followed by another one. All 70 packets arrived.
  • Sharp boundary. Inside the circle everything arrives, one step outside nothing does. UDGM has no transitional region, unlike real links.
  • Only PRR sees lost packets. RSSI and LQI exist only for packets that arrived; the 15 packets lost in experiment C show up only as a gap in the sequence numbers.
